Glossary

Definitions of the terms used on this site. A shared vocabulary keeps documentation consistent: everyone means the same thing by “incident”, “standard” or “runbook”.

ABCD EGIK LMNP RSV

A

Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)
A policy that defines what users may and may not do with an organization's computers, network and internet access.
Access review
A periodic check that every user's access rights are still needed and approved. Unneeded access is removed.
Audit
An independent examination of whether controls, policies and procedures are designed properly and actually followed.
Audit trail
A chronological record (logs, change records, approvals) showing who did what and when.

B

Backout plan
The documented steps to undo a change and return a system to its previous state if the change fails. Also called a rollback plan.
Baseline
The approved, documented configuration of a system, used as a reference for building, auditing and restoring it.
Bring Your Own Device (BYOD)
Allowing employees to use personal devices for work, under rules set in a BYOD policy.

C

Change Advisory Board (CAB)
A group that reviews and approves significant changes to IT systems.
Change management
The process for requesting, assessing, approving, implementing and reviewing changes so they don't cause unplanned outages. ITIL calls it change enablement.
CIA triad
The three goals of information security: Confidentiality, Integrity and Availability.
Classification
A label showing how sensitive information is (e.g. Public, Internal, Confidential, Restricted) and how it must be handled.
Compliance
Meeting the requirements of laws, regulations, contracts, standards and internal policies, and being able to prove it.
Conflict of interest
A situation where personal interests could influence, or appear to influence, professional decisions.

D

Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173)
The Philippine law protecting personal information, enforced by the National Privacy Commission.
Data Protection Officer (DPO)
The person responsible for an organization's compliance with data privacy law.
Docs as code
Writing documentation in plain-text formats (such as Markdown), storing it in version control and reviewing changes like source code.
Document control
The process that makes sure documents are identified, reviewed, approved, versioned and that readers always use the current version.

E

Escalation
Passing an issue to a higher level of expertise or authority when it cannot be solved at the current level or within a set time.

G

Guideline
A recommended practice. Unlike a policy, standard or procedure, a guideline is optional.

I

Incident
An unplanned interruption to a service, a reduction in its quality, or a security event that threatens systems or data.
ISO/IEC 27001
The international standard for information security management systems (ISMS). Organizations can be certified against it.
ITIL
A widely used framework of best practices for IT service management, covering practices such as incident, change and knowledge management.

K

Knowledge base (KB)
A searchable collection of articles that answer common questions and problems.

L

Least privilege
The principle that users and programs get only the minimum access they need to do their job.

M

Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
Signing in with two or more kinds of proof, such as a password plus a code from a phone app.

N

National Privacy Commission (NPC)
The Philippine government agency that administers and enforces the Data Privacy Act.
Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA)
A contract in which a person agrees not to disclose confidential information.

P

Personal data breach
A security incident that leads to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration or disclosure of, or access to, personal data.
Policy
A formal, management-approved statement of rules and their purpose. It says what must be done and why.
Post-incident review (postmortem)
A meeting and report after an incident that examines what happened and how to prevent it, ideally in a blameless way.
Privileged Access Management (PAM)
Tools and processes that control, monitor and record the use of administrator accounts.
Procedure
Step-by-step instructions for carrying out a task. It says how to do it.

R

RACI matrix
A table showing who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted and Informed for each task.
Recovery Point Objective (RPO)
The maximum amount of data, measured in time, that can be lost. An RPO of 24 hours requires at least daily backups.
Recovery Time Objective (RTO)
The maximum time allowed to restore a service after a failure.
Retention policy
Rules for how long each type of data or record is kept, and how it is disposed of.
Root cause analysis (RCA)
A method for finding the underlying cause of a problem, not just its symptoms. The 5 Whys is one RCA technique.
Runbook
A document with the operational steps for running a service and responding to its alerts and failures.

S

Service Level Agreement (SLA)
An agreement that defines the expected level of service, such as availability or response time.
Standard
A mandatory, specific requirement that supports a policy, e.g. “passwords must be at least 12 characters”.
Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)
A documented, approved procedure for a routine task, followed the same way every time.
Style guide
A set of writing and formatting rules that keeps documents consistent.

V

Version control
Tracking changes to files over time so earlier versions can be compared or restored. Git is the most common tool.
Virtual LAN (VLAN)
A logically separate network on shared switches, used to isolate groups of devices such as servers, staff and guests.
