File Server Outage: Disk Full on fs-01

On 2026-10-06, staff could not open or save files on the shared drives for 85 minutes because a debug log filled the server's disk.

Blameless report

This report focuses on systems and processes, not on blaming individuals. Its goal is to understand what happened and prevent it from happening again.

Summary

On 6 October 2026, from 08:40 to 10:05 Philippine Time, staff in four departments could not open or save files on the shared network drives hosted on fs-01. The /var partition reached 100% capacity because a Samba debug log had grown to 38 GB. The log level had been raised to the maximum during troubleshooting four days earlier and was never set back. Service was restored by archiving the log, restoring the normal log level and restarting Samba. No data was lost.

Impact

  • Users affected: about 120 staff (Finance, HR, Sales and Operations).
  • Business impact: 85 minutes without shared files; the payroll team's cut-off was moved by two hours.
  • Data: no data lost or corrupted; files saved before 08:40 were intact.
  • Security / privacy: no unauthorized access or disclosure (see privacy assessment).

Detection

The incident was detected by users, seven minutes after it began. Monitoring did not alert because disk-space alerts were not configured on fs-01. This is a detection gap (see action 1).

Root cause analysis

We used the 5 Whys technique, asking “why?” until we reached a cause we can fix:

  1. Why were the shares unavailable?Samba could not write to /var.
  2. Why could it not write?The /var partition was 100% full.
  3. Why was it full?The Samba log grew to 38 GB in four days.
  4. Why did the log grow so fast?Debug logging (level 10) was left on after troubleshooting.
  5. Why was it left on?The temporary change was not documented, so no one had a task to revert it. Log rotation had no size limit, and there was no disk-space alert.

Root cause

An undocumented temporary configuration change, combined with no size limit on log rotation and no disk-usage monitoring.

Resolution

Commands run during recovery, recorded for future reference:

df -h /var                                             # confirmed 100% used
sudo du -xh /var --max-depth=3 | sort -h | tail -n 5   # found log.smbd (38G)
sudo systemctl stop smbd
sudo rsync -a /var/log/samba/log.smbd backup@bkp-01:/incidents/INC-2026-0142/
sudo truncate -s 0 /var/log/samba/log.smbd
sudo sed -i 's/log level = 10/log level = 1/' /etc/samba/smb.conf
testparm -s 2>/dev/null | grep "log level"             # verify the new setting
sudo systemctl start smbd

Data privacy assessment

The incident affected availability only. No personal data was accessed by unauthorized people, disclosed, altered or lost. It is therefore not a reportable personal data breach under the Data Privacy Act of 2012. The Data Protection Officer reviewed this assessment and agreed on 2026-10-07.

Why record this?

Even when no notification is needed, writing down the assessment and who approved it shows regulators and auditors that the question was considered. See Data Privacy Act.

Lessons learned

What went well

  • Clear escalation: the incident was declared within 15 minutes.
  • The cause was found quickly with standard commands.
  • Users received a clear resolution notice.

What we will improve

  • Temporary changes must be documented, even when they seem small.
  • Monitoring must alert us before users notice.
  • Status updates should go out during the incident, not only after.
